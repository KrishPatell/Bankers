PRP vs Steroid Injection vs Embolization for Knee Pain: Which Treatment Is Right for You?
Knee pain is one of the most common problems affecting adults, particularly people with knee osteoarthritis, sports injuries, cartilage degeneration, and chronic inflammation. When pain starts interfering with walking, climbing stairs, exercising, or performing everyday activities, patients often look for treatments that can provide relief without immediately undergoing surgery.
Today, several minimally invasive options are available. Among them, Platelet-Rich Plasma (PRP), corticosteroid injections, and Genicular Artery Embolization (GAE) are increasingly discussed for appropriate patients.
But these treatments are not the same.
They work in different ways, are suited to different situations, and have different advantages and limitations.
So, the important question is not simply:
“Which treatment is best?”
It is:
“Which treatment is most appropriate for the cause and severity of my knee pain?”

What Is PRP for Knee Pain?
Platelet-Rich Plasma (PRP) therapy uses a patient's own blood.
A small blood sample is collected and processed to concentrate platelets and other components of plasma. The resulting PRP is then injected into the affected area.
The concept behind PRP is different from simply suppressing pain. Platelets contain biologically active substances that may influence inflammation and tissue-repair processes.
PRP has been studied particularly in knee osteoarthritis, although results vary according to the type of PRP preparation, patient characteristics, severity of disease, and treatment protocol.

Potential advantages of PRP
Uses the patient's own blood
Minimally invasive
Does not require surgery
Can be performed as an outpatient procedure
May provide longer-lasting symptom improvement than some short-term injection options in selected patients
May be combined with rehabilitation and physiotherapy
However, PRP should not be described as a guaranteed method of rebuilding severely damaged cartilage or permanently curing osteoarthritis.

What Is a Steroid Injection?
A corticosteroid injection delivers an anti-inflammatory medication directly into or around the knee joint.
Steroids can reduce inflammation and may provide relatively rapid improvement in pain for some patients.
This can be particularly useful when inflammation is contributing significantly to symptoms.

Potential advantages
Relatively quick symptom relief
Minimally invasive
Usually performed on an outpatient basis
Can be useful when inflammation is prominent
May help patients participate more comfortably in rehabilitation
However, corticosteroid injections are generally considered a symptom-management treatment, rather than a treatment that reverses the underlying degenerative process of osteoarthritis.
Repeated injections also need to be considered carefully. The appropriate frequency depends on the individual patient and clinical circumstances.

What Is Genicular Artery Embolization?
Genicular Artery Embolization (GAE) is fundamentally different from PRP and steroid injections.
Instead of injecting medication or biological material into the knee, GAE is performed using a small catheter placed into an artery.
Using imaging guidance, an interventional specialist identifies abnormal blood vessels associated with the inflammatory environment around the knee and delivers tiny embolic particles to reduce blood flow in selected abnormal vessels.
The treatment is based on the observation that chronic knee osteoarthritis can be associated with abnormal synovial vascularity and inflammation.
Recent research suggests GAE may provide clinically meaningful pain improvement in selected patients, although the quality and consistency of evidence remain areas of ongoing research. A 2026 systematic review and meta-analysis involving 45 studies and 2,205 patients reported improvements in pain and function, but rated the overall certainty of evidence as low to very low because much of the literature consists of uncontrolled studies.

How Do the Three Treatments Work Differently?
Understanding the mechanism makes the comparison much easier.
PRP
Biological approach
PRP introduces concentrated platelets and plasma components into the treatment area with the aim of influencing the local healing and inflammatory environment.
Steroid Injection
Anti-inflammatory approach
Corticosteroids suppress inflammatory activity and can provide relatively rapid symptom relief.
GAE
Vascular approach
GAE targets abnormal blood vessels associated with chronic synovial inflammation and pain.
In simple terms:
PRP → biological treatment
Steroid → anti-inflammatory treatment
GAE → vascular/interventional treatment

PRP vs Steroid Injection
PRP and steroid injections are both delivered by injection, but their goals are different.
Steroid injections primarily focus on reducing inflammation quickly, whereas PRP is intended to influence the biological environment of the joint.
Some studies suggest PRP may provide more sustained improvement than corticosteroid injections in certain patients with knee osteoarthritis, although results differ between studies and treatment protocols.
Therefore, a steroid injection may be considered when relatively rapid short-term symptom control is important, while PRP may be considered when a patient and clinician are looking for a different minimally invasive strategy for longer-term symptom management.
The choice depends on the patient's diagnosis, severity of symptoms, previous treatments, and overall medical condition.

PRP vs Genicular Artery Embolization
This comparison is particularly interesting because both are minimally invasive but work through completely different mechanisms.
PRP
PRP is an injection-based biological treatment.
It may be considered for selected patients with symptomatic knee conditions, particularly certain cases of osteoarthritis.
GAE
GAE is a catheter-based vascular procedure.
It is primarily being investigated and used for selected patients with chronic symptomatic knee osteoarthritis, particularly those who continue to experience significant pain despite conservative treatment.
GAE does not inject PRP or steroid medication into the joint.
Instead, the procedure targets abnormal genicular arterial blood supply associated with the inflammatory process.
More recent research continues to show promise but also emphasizes the need for stronger randomized evidence.
Steroid Injection vs Genicular Artery Embolization
These treatments may both be considered for people experiencing chronic knee pain, but they are very different procedures.
A steroid injection places medication into the knee region to reduce inflammation.
GAE uses angiography and a catheter to identify and embolize selected abnormal vessels.
Steroid injection may be considered when:
Rapid symptom relief is desired
Inflammation is an important component of the pain
A less complex injection-based treatment is appropriate
GAE may be considered when:
Knee osteoarthritis is causing persistent pain
Conservative treatments have not provided sufficient relief
The patient is an appropriate candidate for a vascular intervention
A minimally invasive alternative is being considered before surgery
Importantly, GAE should not be presented as automatically superior to steroid injections. Current comparative evidence remains limited, and patient selection is crucial.

Which Treatment Provides Faster Relief?
Generally, corticosteroid injections can provide faster symptom relief than PRP because their primary action is anti-inflammatory.
PRP often works more gradually.
GAE also does not necessarily produce immediate relief. Improvement can develop over the following weeks as the inflammatory environment changes.
The exact response varies significantly between individuals.
Therefore:
Fast short-term relief → Steroid may be considered
Gradual biological approach → PRP may be considered
Persistent OA pain with a vascular/inflammatory target → GAE may be considered
These are general concepts, not rules for every patient.


Which Treatment Is Best for Severe Knee Osteoarthritis?
Severity matters.
Patients with advanced osteoarthritis may have substantial structural changes, including significant cartilage loss and bone changes.
No injection or embolization procedure should be promoted as a guaranteed replacement for knee replacement surgery in advanced disease.
However, selected patients who are not ready for surgery, are not suitable surgical candidates, or want to explore minimally invasive options may be evaluated for treatments such as PRP or GAE depending on their specific condition.
Interestingly, research has suggested that patients with advanced OA and degenerative MRI findings may have lower rates of clinically meaningful improvement after GAE than patients with less advanced disease.
This highlights an important point:
The earlier and more accurately a patient is assessed, the better the treatment decision can be individualized.

Can These Treatments Help Avoid Knee Replacement?
This is one of the most common questions.
The answer is sometimes they may help delay the need for surgery, but none should be marketed as a guaranteed way to avoid knee replacement.
PRP may reduce symptoms and improve function in appropriately selected patients.
Steroid injections may provide temporary symptom relief.
GAE is being studied as a minimally invasive option that may help selected patients manage chronic knee OA pain and potentially delay more invasive treatment.
But if the joint has advanced structural damage and symptoms remain severe despite appropriate treatment, knee replacement may eventually become the most appropriate option.
Can PRP, Steroid Injection and GAE Be Combined?
Treatment should not be thought of as a competition where one procedure must replace all others.
A patient's treatment pathway can involve different approaches at different stages.
For example, a patient may first undergo:
Lifestyle modification → Physiotherapy → Medication → Injection-based treatment → Interventional treatment → Surgery if necessary
The appropriate pathway depends on the individual.
GAE, PRP, and steroid injections should therefore be viewed as different tools for different clinical situations, rather than three identical alternatives.

GAE Treatment at Bankers Vascular Hospital
At Bankers Vascular Hospital, the focus is on minimally invasive and image-guided treatment options for patients who may benefit from alternatives to conventional surgery.
For appropriately selected patients with chronic knee osteoarthritis and persistent pain, Genicular Artery Embolization (GAE) may be considered.
The procedure is performed by accessing an artery through a small catheter and using imaging guidance to identify the vessels supplying the abnormal inflammatory region around the knee. Selected vessels are then embolized using appropriate embolic material.
The goal is to reduce abnormal vascularity associated with the inflammatory pain process while avoiding an open surgical procedure.
Because GAE is an evolving treatment, appropriate patient selection and specialist evaluation are particularly important. Current research supports potential benefit in selected patients but also shows that evidence is still developing.

PRP vs Steroid vs Embolization: The Bottom Line
There is no single treatment that is best for every person with knee pain.
PRP may be suitable for selected patients looking for a biological, minimally invasive treatment approach.
Steroid injections can be useful when short-term reduction of inflammation and pain is the primary goal.
Genicular Artery Embolization offers a different, catheter-based approach that targets abnormal genicular arterial blood flow and is being increasingly studied for chronic knee osteoarthritis.
The key is to identify what is causing the knee pain, how advanced the disease is, and what treatments have already been tried.
A simple way to remember the difference:
PRP: “Use the patient's own blood components to support a biological response.”
Steroid: “Reduce inflammation and provide symptom relief.”
GAE: “Target abnormal blood vessels associated with chronic knee inflammation and pain.”
For patients who want to explore minimally invasive options, a detailed evaluation by an experienced specialist can help determine whether PRP, steroid injection, GAE, physiotherapy, medication, or surgery is the most appropriate next step.
